**Goal:** A hero standing at any stairs landing can leave the crawl without
ending it, and the town's Enter Dungeon door forks into Resume or Delve anew

```dart
/// The profile [entered] became, with [state] left standing where it is.
///
/// **A homecoming for the hero, and nothing at all for the dungeon.** Leaving
/// alive and leaving to come back carry the identical set home — the same hit
/// points, gear, training, pack, purse and visit `endRun` carries — because what
/// the hero is does not depend on whether they mean to return. What differs is
/// the other half: [endRun] is the last anyone sees of the crawl, while this
/// leaves it whole for [resumeRun] to walk back into. Nothing here touches
/// [state]; the caller keeps it and is the one that has to write it down.
///
/// Heals nothing, for [endRun]'s reason: the inn is what heals, and a free cure
/// at the door would make it decoration.
///
/// **After this the profile and the crawl agree about the visit**, because the
/// visit comes home with everything else. That is not bookkeeping — it is the
/// precondition [resumeRun] needs, and the reason resuming can be well-defined
/// at all.
///
/// Must not be called on a game-over state: a dead hero has no camp to come
/// back to, and this would carry the corpse home with its pack intact. The gate
/// is the interface's, exactly as it is for [endRun] — the stairs offer leaving
/// only while the hero is alive — and stating the contract here rather than
/// asserting it keeps one gate instead of two that can disagree.
Profile suspendRun(Profile entered, GameState state) => entered.copyWith(
  hero: state.hero,
  equipment: state.equipment,
  skills: state.skills,
  inventory: state.inventory,
  gold: state.gold,
  visit: state.visit,
);
```

```dart
/// The crawl [suspended] was, with [profile] walking back into it.
///
/// **Everything the hero is comes from the profile; everything the dungeon is
/// comes from the suspended block.** Those are exactly the two halves
/// [suspendRun] separated, put back together in the other direction — so a
/// potion bought while camped is in the pack, an inn bed shows in the hit
/// points, and a level bought at the trainer is trained, while the floor, the
/// monsters, the fog, the litter, the clock and both random streams are the
/// ones the hero walked away from.
///
/// The hero is the suspended actor wearing the profile's hit points, rather
/// than the profile's actor outright. Both would read the same today — nothing
/// in town moves a hero — but only one of them says which half owns where the
/// hero is standing, and a town that one day moved a position would teleport
/// the player rather than reddening a test.
///
/// Banked gold stays banked. The vault is not part of a run, which is the whole
/// reason it is not on the state, and a resume that carried it down would put
/// the bank inside the thing death takes.
///
/// **Resuming is not entering, so the visit is not bumped and the dungeon does
/// not reshuffle** — the argument `loadRun` makes about a relaunch, holding for
/// the same reason about a walk back down the same stairs. Delving anew is what
/// reshuffles, and it goes through `startRun` like every other entry.
///
/// Requires `profile.visit == suspended.visit`, which [suspendRun] establishes
/// and nothing in town disturbs. Stated rather than asserted, for
/// [suspendRun]'s reason.
GameState resumeRun(Profile profile, GameState suspended) => GameState(
  map: suspended.map,
  hero: suspended.hero.copyWith(hp: profile.hero.hp),
  monsters: suspended.monsters,
  rng: suspended.rng,
  lootRng: suspended.lootRng,
  visible: suspended.visible,
  explored: suspended.explored,
  buildFloor: suspended.buildFloor,
  depth: suspended.depth,
  worldSeed: suspended.worldSeed,
  visit: suspended.visit,
  stairsDown: suspended.stairsDown,
  stairsUp: suspended.stairsUp,
  gold: profile.gold,
  isGameOver: suspended.isGameOver,
  floors: suspended.floors,
  groundItems: suspended.groundItems,
  inventory: profile.inventory,
  equipment: profile.equipment,
  skills: profile.skills,
  dropTables: suspended.dropTables,
  nextDropNumber: suspended.nextDropNumber,
);
```

```dart
  test('suspending and resuming with no town business in between is the '

    // act
    final (livePlayed, liveEvents) = _play(live);
    final (resumedPlayed, resumedEvents) = _play(resumed);
```

`TownViewState` gains:

```dart
  /// The crawl waiting to be walked back into, or null when there is no camp.
  ///
  /// **Not [run], and the two must never be the same field.** [run] is an
  /// instruction — "open this crawl now" — read once by the session and then
  /// gone; this is a fact about where the hero's dungeon is, and it survives
  /// every transaction in town. Sharing one field would make entering the
  /// dungeon indistinguishable from having somewhere to go back to, and the
  /// screen could not tell which door to draw.

  /// a crawl the player is standing three floors inside, and a handler that
  /// dropped it would have the autosaver write `run: null` on the next purchase
  /// and erase the dungeon from disk with nothing on screen to say so.
  ///

  /// and both doors out of a camp clear this as they set that.
  final GameState? suspended;
```

```dart
  /// Brings the hero home and leaves the crawl standing where it is.
  ///
  /// The visit state goes, and it has to. Suspending carries the run's visit
  /// home with everything else, so the shelf is rolled again from the new
  /// number — and what was bought off the old shelf names ids that no longer
  /// exist, while what is on the counter was sold to a shop that has since
  /// turned over its stock. Leaving those lists in place would put a dead id on
  /// the counter at a price the merchant would honour.
  void _onRunSuspended(RunSuspended event, Emitter<TownViewState> emit) {
    final home = suspendRun(state.profile, event.state);
    emit(
      TownViewState(
        profile: home,
        stock: merchantStock(home.worldSeed, home.visit),
        suspended: event.state,
      ),
    );
  }

  /// Walks back into the camp, with everything town did since folded in.
  void _onResumeCrawl(ResumeCrawlPressed event, Emitter<TownViewState> emit) {
    if (state.suspended case final GameState camp) {
      emit(
        TownViewState(
          profile: state.profile,
          stock: state.stock,
          merchant: state.merchant,
          run: resumeRun(state.profile, camp),
        ),
      );
    }
  }

  /// Gives the camp up and walks into a dungeon laid out afresh.
  ///
  /// **Nothing is lost by it.** Suspending already brought the hero's hit
  /// points, gear, training, pack and purse home, so the camp holds a floor
  /// plan and a pair of stream states and nothing the player earned. What it
  /// costs is the depth reached, which is why the interface asks first.
  ///
  /// `startDungeonRun` bumps the visit by construction, and that bump IS the
  /// reshuffle — which is what keeps the farming loop alive now that leaving no
  /// longer ends a run.
  void _onDelveAnew(DelveAnewPressed event, Emitter<TownViewState> emit) => emit(
    TownViewState(
      profile: state.profile,
      stock: state.stock,
      merchant: state.merchant,
      run: startDungeonRun(state.profile),
    ),
  );
```

The control calls `suspendDungeon`; the death overlay keeps `leaveDungeon`.

```dart
/// Leaves the crawl standing and uncovers the town screen.
///
/// The town was never torn down — entering pushed the crawl on top of it — so
/// walking out is one pop and one event, exactly as [leaveDungeon] is, and there
/// is one place in the app that does each.
///
/// The stairs are the door this goes through and death is the other one's, which
/// is the whole difference between them: leaving at a landing is an interruption
/// and the dungeon waits, while dying is an ending and the dungeon is rolled
/// again. Both hand the same [GameState] over; only the town decides what it
/// means.
void suspendDungeon(BuildContext context, GameViewState state) {
  context.read<TownBloc>().add(RunSuspended(state.game));
  Navigator.of(context).pop();
}
```

and `GameScreen`'s `PopScope` rationale is rewritten: the stale sentence says a
pop would leave the run un-ended, and the reason is now that the back button is
not the stairs. Suspending is a decision made at a landing, so a pop from the
middle of a floor is still not an exit.
